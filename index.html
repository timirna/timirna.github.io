Case Study

From One Reference to a Living Character: The Koala Sleep Tracker Mascot

Character Design · Rigging · Interactive Animation — built in Adobe Illustrator & Rive
By Timir — Motion Designer & 2D Animator

Overview

Koala Sleep Tracker is a character-led sleep companion app for iOS. Instead of dry charts and numbers, the app expresses your sleep journey through a koala mascot that reacts to how you sleep — getting drowsy when it's time to wind down, celebrating when you hit your goals.

My job was to make that character exist: design it from scratch, rig it, and bring it to life as a real-time interactive animation that runs natively inside the app.

The Challenge

The developer came to me with a single reference image and a vision: the koala shouldn't be decoration — it should be the app experience. That meant the character needed a wide emotional range, smooth transitions between moods, and a file light enough to run in real time on mobile. Pre-rendered video was never an option; every state had to be interactive and instant.

Design: One Reference, Full Master Illustration

Working in Adobe Illustrator, I developed the single reference into a complete master illustration — a fully resolved character with personality, proportions, and appeal that could carry an entire app.

I designed the koala as clean flat vector from the start. This was a deliberate choice, not a style shortcut: flat vector keeps file sizes tiny, scales perfectly across screen sizes, and — most importantly — every shape was built with rigging in mind. Limbs, ears, eyelids, and facial features were constructed as separate, animation-ready pieces rather than a single flattened drawing.

Detailing in Rive: Shadow, Color, Depth

Rather than baking shadows and highlights into the Illustrator file, I brought the flat vector into Rive and did the detailing there — shadows, color refinement, everything. Doing this inside Rive means the depth and shading move with the character during animation instead of sitting frozen on top of it. The result is a character that feels dimensional and alive while staying 100% vector and lightweight.

Rigging & The Unified State Machine

I rigged the character with bones and constraints for clean, reusable motion, then built nine animation states: Idle, Talking, Sleepy, Tired, Sleeping, Wake Up, Celebration, Crying, and Pride.

The hardest engineering problem came here. The states originally lived across multiple artboards — and I consolidated all of them into one unified state machine on a single artboard. One artboard, one brain: the koala now flows naturally between any mood the app calls for, with smooth transitions and no duplicated assets.

Delivery

The final deliverable was a single lightweight .riv file that the developer drops straight into the iOS app. It runs in real time, responds to app logic, and weighs a fraction of what video assets would — the entire app is just over 10 MB.

What I Learned

Plan the state machine before you animate. Consolidating multiple artboards into a unified state machine after the fact is a puzzle you only want to solve once — designing the architecture first is what makes a nine-state character feel effortless instead of tangled.

Results

A complete character pipeline — from a single reference image to a shipped, interactive mascot — designed in Illustrator, detailed and rigged in Rive, and delivered as one production-ready file.
